How to Use the Passive Income Calculator

Read time: 8 min · Published: February 25, 2026 · Updated: November 7, 2026

If you want to achieve financial independence, plan your investments, and find out how much you need to invest to live off passive income, the Passive Income Calculator is the ideal tool. In this article, we'll walk you through it step by step with examples using this month's reference rates and detailed explanations.

1. What is Passive Income and Why Does It Matter?

Passive income is money you receive regularly without actively working for it. It can come from rentals, dividends, interest on investments, or royalties. By building a solid passive income stream, you can achieve financial freedom, reduce your dependence on a traditional job, and gain more autonomy over your time.

2. What the Calculator Answers

The calculator starts with one question: what do you want to find out? There are four options, and only the fields each one needs are shown:

  • How much to invest monthly: enter the income you want, your current savings and the time frame; it calculates the monthly contribution.
  • How long it will take: enter the income you want, your current savings and your monthly contribution; it calculates the time.
  • How much I will have: enter your current savings, monthly contribution and time frame; it calculates your final balance and the income it pays.
  • How much I need: enter only the income you want; it calculates the balance you need invested.

Everything uses monthly compounding, with contributions added at the end of each month. Passive income is what your balance earns each month without spending the principal.

3. Step by Step

  1. Choose what you want to find out (one of the four options above).
  2. Choose where you will invest: high-yield savings account, 1-year CD, Treasury bills, dividend ETF, S&P 500 index fund, or Custom rate.
  3. Check the annual return: the field is pre-filled with the reference rate. The badge below it tells you its status:
    • Green — Updated: market rate confirmed this month (U.S. Treasury). Rates are updated automatically every month.
    • Amber — Average estimate: savings, CD and ETF rates vary by bank and fund, so we use an average. If you know your rate, type it in.
    • Amber — May be out of date: the rate could not be updated recently. Check it and adjust if needed.
    • Gray — Historical average: the S&P 500 uses its long-term average. Lower it to be more conservative.
    • Purple — Your own rate: shown when you type a different value. The investment's tax estimate still applies, and Back to the reference rate undoes the change.
  4. Fill in the amounts the question needs: desired passive income, current savings, monthly contribution and time frame in years.
  5. Pick your options:
    • Subtract taxes (on by default): an estimate — about 22% on interest (savings, CDs, T-bills) and 15% on qualified dividends and long-term gains.
    • Show in today's money: subtracts inflation, so every amount is shown in today's purchasing power — the most honest way to plan for the long term.
  6. Click Calculate: the card flips and shows your result.
Tip: run the same simulation with and without today's money to see what inflation does to your plan. Open the Calculator

4. Examples with November 2026 Rates

These examples use November 2026 reference rates (T-bills 4.11%, dividend ETF yield 3.7%, S&P 500 long-term average 10%, inflation 3.7%). When you open the calculator you will see the current month's rates, so the numbers may differ.

Example 1: How much to invest for $3,000 a month in 20 years

  • Question: How much to invest monthly
  • Investment: S&P 500 index fund, taxes subtracted
  • Desired income: $3,000/month · Current savings: $0 · Time frame: 20 years

Result: about $739 a month, building $443,000. Now turn on Show in today's money: the contribution rises to about $2,074 a month and the balance to $803,000. That gap is inflation — $3,000 twenty years from now buys much less than today. To keep today's purchasing power, the second number is the one that counts.

Example 2: How long until $2,000 a month

  • Question: How long it will take
  • Investment: S&P 500 index fund, taxes subtracted, in today's money
  • Desired income: $2,000 · Current savings: $20,000 · Monthly contribution: $1,000

Result: about 23 years, with a balance of $537,000 in today's dollars.

Example 3: How much a dividend ETF portfolio will pay

  • Question: How much I will have
  • Investment: Dividend ETF (3.7% average yield), taxes subtracted
  • Current savings: $10,000 · Monthly contribution: $700 · Time frame: 10 years

Result: about $112,000 accumulated, paying around $288 a month in dividends after tax. The calculator counts only the dividends, not price changes of the ETF.

Example 4: How much you need for $5,000 a month

  • Question: How much I need
  • Investment: Treasury bills, taxes subtracted
  • Desired income: $5,000/month

Result: about $1.91 million. That income would not grow with inflation, though — compare it with the 4% rule in how much to invest to make $5,000 a month.

5. How to Interpret the Results

The result screen shows the answer to your question at the top — for example, "To earn $3,000/month in 20 years, invest $739/month". Below it:

  • Your choices: the investment, whether taxes were subtracted, whether amounts are in today's money, and where the rate came from (reference or entered by you).
  • Return used: the monthly and annual rate used in the calculation — after taxes and above inflation when those options are on.
  • Total invested: current savings + all contributions.
  • Earnings: the difference between the final balance and the total invested.
  • Final balance and monthly passive income: what you will have and how much it pays each month.
  • A chart of your balance over time and a card ready to share.

Remember: this is an educational simulation using reference rates that change over time. It is not investment advice or a guarantee of returns.

6. Best Practices for Building Passive Income

  • Reinvest returns: Reinvesting profits exponentially accelerates your wealth growth.
  • Be consistent: Discipline matters more than the amount invested. Consistency builds results.
  • Diversify: Spread across index funds, REITs, dividend stocks, and bonds to reduce risk.
  • Account for inflation: Always think in terms of real returns (above inflation), especially over long periods.
  • Review your plan periodically: Adjust periods, rates, and contributions as your situation changes.
