Interest vs. the 4% Rule

Living only on interest keeps your principal intact, but cash rates move with the Federal Reserve: when rates fall, your income falls too. The 4% rule takes a different approach — invest in a diversified stock and bond portfolio and withdraw 4% in the first year, then adjust for inflation. On $250,000 that's about $833 per month, and historically it has lasted at least 30 years in most scenarios.

Is $250,000 Enough to Retire?

The average American household spends about $6,440 per month. A $250,000 portfolio is not enough to retire on by itself, but it is a solid income supplement and a strong base to keep compounding. Add Social Security, a pension or part-time income and the picture improves quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much interest does $250,000 earn per month?

In a high-yield savings account paying 4.20% APY, $250,000 earns about $875 per month ($10,500 a year) before taxes. A 1-year CD at 4.40% pays about $917 per month.

Can I live off the interest of $250,000?

Using the 4% rule, $250,000 supports about $833 per month, adjusted for inflation every year. The average U.S. household spends about $6,440 per month, so $250,000 cannot cover average spending on its own.

What is the safest way to earn monthly income on $250,000?

High-yield savings accounts and CDs are FDIC insured up to $250,000 per depositor per bank, and Treasury bills are backed by the U.S. government.

How much will $250,000 be worth in 10 years?

At the stock market's long-term average of 10% a year, $250,000 would grow to about $648,436 in 10 years — about $491,788 in today's dollars after inflation. Returns are not guaranteed.
