How Much Do I Need to Invest to Make $6,000 a Month?

Read time: 5 min · Updated October 2026

To make $6,000 a month ($72,000 a year) in passive income, you need roughly $1,800,000 invested using the 4% rule — or $1,636,364 if you want to live only on CD interest at today's rates. Here is the full breakdown with October 2026 rates.

The Formula

Amount needed = Monthly income × 12 ÷ yield

$6,000 × 12 ÷ 4% = $1,800,000

The lower the yield you assume, the bigger the target — but the safer your income. Cash yields look attractive today, but they follow the Federal Reserve. The 4% rule assumes a stock-heavy portfolio that grows with inflation.

Ways to Get There Faster

  • Max out tax-advantaged accounts — 401(k) match first, then a Roth IRA (see 401(k) vs Roth IRA).
  • Raise contributions with every raise — increasing them 5–10% a year cuts years off the timeline.
  • Reinvest dividends during the accumulation phase.
  • Lower your target — every $100 less in monthly spending reduces the goal by $30,000.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do I need to invest to make $6,000 a month?

Using the 4% rule you need about $1,800,000. If you only want to live on interest from a high-yield savings account at 4.20%, you need about $1,714,286, and with a dividend ETF yielding ~3.7%, about $1,945,946.

How long does it take to make $6,000 a month in passive income?

Investing $1,000 a month at 7% after inflation, you reach $1,800,000 in about 35 years and 9 months. With $2,000 a month it takes about 26 years and 9 months.

Is $6,000 a month enough to retire?

$6,000 a month is $72,000 a year. The average U.S. household spends about $6,440 a month, so $6,000 works best combined with Social Security or other income.
