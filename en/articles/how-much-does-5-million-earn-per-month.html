Interest vs. the 4% Rule

Living only on interest keeps your principal intact, but cash rates move with the Federal Reserve: when rates fall, your income falls too. The 4% rule takes a different approach — invest in a diversified stock and bond portfolio and withdraw 4% in the first year, then adjust for inflation. On $5 million that's about $16,667 per month, and historically it has lasted at least 30 years in most scenarios.

Is $5 million Enough to Retire?

The average American household spends about $6,440 per month. A $5 million portfolio covers the average American household's spending with plenty of room to spare — a realistic early-retirement number for most people. Add Social Security, a pension or part-time income and the picture improves quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much interest does $5 million earn per month?

In a high-yield savings account paying 4.20% APY, $5 million earns about $17,500 per month ($210,000 a year) before taxes. A 1-year CD at 4.40% pays about $18,333 per month.

Can I live off the interest of $5 million?

Using the 4% rule, $5 million supports about $16,667 per month, adjusted for inflation every year. The average U.S. household spends about $6,440 per month, so $5 million can cover average spending on its own.

What is the safest way to earn monthly income on $5 million?

High-yield savings accounts and CDs are FDIC insured up to $250,000 per depositor per bank, and Treasury bills are backed by the U.S. government. With $5 million, spread cash across several banks or use Treasuries to stay fully protected.

How much will $5 million be worth in 10 years?

At the stock market's long-term average of 10% a year, $5 million would grow to about $12,968,712 in 10 years — about $9,835,757 in today's dollars after inflation. Returns are not guaranteed.
