How Long Does It Take to Save $1 Million Investing $200 a Month?

Read time: 5 min · Updated October 2026

Investing $200 a month at the stock market's long-term average of 10% a year, you reach $1 million in about 39 years. Here's how the timeline changes with different returns, starting ages and goals.

Time to $1 Million Investing $200 a Month

Annual returnTime to $1 millionYou contributeCompound growth

Reference rates (October 2026): high-yield savings 4.20% APY, 1-year CD 4.40%, 3-month T-bills 4.20%, dividend ETF yield ~3.7%, stock market long-term average 10% a year (7% after inflation). Figures are before taxes: interest is taxed as ordinary income, qualified dividends at lower rates. Sources: CNBC Select and NerdWallet (savings and CD rates, Oct 2026), U.S. Treasury (3-month bill), BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey (household spending). Not financial advice.

How to Get There Faster

  • Use a 401(k) match — it's an instant 50–100% return on your contribution.
  • Keep costs low — broad index funds charge 0.03–0.10% a year.
  • Increase $200 over time — raising it 5% a year cuts years off the timeline.
  • Stay invested in down markets — buying during drops is what makes the average work.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to save $1 million investing $200 a month?

At the stock market's long-term average of 10% a year, investing $200 a month gets you to $1 million in about 39 years. At a more conservative 6%, it takes about 55 years and 6 months.

How much of the $1 million is my own money?

At 10% a year you contribute about $93,600; the remaining $906,400 comes from compound growth.

What is $1 million worth after inflation?

Measured in today's dollars (7% real return), reaching $1 million of purchasing power takes about 49 years and 11 months investing $200 a month.

How much monthly income does $1 million produce?

With the 4% rule, $1 million supports about $3,333 a month. In a high-yield savings account at 4.20%, it earns about $3,500 a month in interest.
